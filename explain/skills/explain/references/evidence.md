# Evidence behind the directives

Keyed on the directive as it appears in `SKILL.md`, in that order. Each bullet: finding — citation — replication strength; applicability or support limit.

`WR` well-replicated (multiple labs or meta-analytic). `MOD` consistent but narrow or heavily moderated. `DISP` disputed or failed replication. A framework is marked as such rather than graded.

Resolving a conflict between two directives: applicability to the case in front of you comes before replication strength. A well-replicated finding outside its conditions does not defeat a narrower one inside them, and a robust finding does not validate every instruction derived from it.

### Fix the reader model first; where the reader already holds something, state the delta

- Worked examples, integrated text, scaffolds and segmentation go from positive to negative effect as prior knowledge rises — the support becomes redundancy the reader must process and suppress — Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003), *Educational Psychologist* 38(1), 23–31; Kalyuga (2007), *Educ Psych Rev* 19, 509–539 — WR.
- Mechanism: content the reader has a schema for has low element interactivity, so its scaffold is pure extraneous load — Chen, Kalyuga & Sweller (2017), *Educ Psych Rev* 29, 393–405 — WR.
- The explainer's own estimate of the reader is unreliable, and warnings do not debias it; decomposing the task into steps does — Hinds (1999), *JEP: Applied* 5(2); Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber (1989); Birch & Bloom (2007), *Psych Science* — WR (curse of knowledge), MOD (expert blind spot). This is why the coordinates are read off evidence rather than predicted.

### Schema — assess the concrete target separately from its concepts; a term used correctly is evidence about that term

- Expert advantage is over *meaningful* configurations and vanishes on random ones; experts sort problems by deep principle, novices by surface feature — Chase & Simon (1973), *Cognitive Psychology* 4; Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981), *Cognitive Science* 5 — WR. Supports domain-specific chunking; it does not establish that correct use of one term implies the surrounding schema.

### Schema — without it, one element per unit; with it, name the pattern and stop

- Difficulty tracks how many elements must be held simultaneously because they interact, not volume — Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas (1998, 2019), *Educ Psych Rev* — WR.
- Segmenting d = 0.67, 7/7 — Mayer (2021), *Multimedia Learning* 3rd ed. — WR, with a known reversal at high prior knowledge (Spanjers et al. 2011).

### Foil — recover what the reader expected; give the few causes that separate actual from expected

- Explanation is contrastive ("why P rather than Q", Q usually implicit), selected (one or two causes, not the chain), and social; statistics satisfy less than causes — Lipton (1990), *Contrastive Explanation*; Hilton (1990), *Psych Bulletin* 107; Miller (2019), *Artificial Intelligence* 267, 1–38 — WR as a descriptive claim about what people want. It does not establish that every request has a recoverable foil, which is why the no-foil branch exists.

### Level — answer where the question was posed, name the level, descend only as required

- Computational / algorithmic / realizational — Marr (1982), *Vision*, ch. 1 — analytic framework, not an experimental finding; criticized for implying the levels are independent (Bechtel & Shagrir 2015).
- Logically irrelevant lower-level *verbal* detail raises judged explanation quality — Hopkins, Weisberg & Taylor (2016), *Cognition* 155, 67–76; Fernandez-Duque et al. (2015), *J Cog Neurosci* — MOD.
- The corresponding brain-*image* effect failed a ~2,000-participant replication — Michael et al. (2013), *Psychon Bull Rev* — DISP. Distinguish irrelevant verbal detail from images.

### Wrong model — name it, state it fairly, say where it fails, then give the right one

- Refutation text outperforms plain expository text, which was the least effective strategy tested; the proposed mechanism is co-activation of the old and new conception — Guzzetti, Snyder, Glass & Gamas (1993), *Reading Research Quarterly* 28(2); Schroeder & Kucera (2022), *Educ Psych Rev* 34(2), 957–987 (g = 0.41, 44 comparisons, n = 3,869); Kendeou & van den Broek (2007) — WR.
- Stating only the correct account lets the reader graft it onto an intact wrong framework — a synthetic model — Vosniadou & Brewer (1992), *Cognitive Psychology* 24 — MOD.
- Applicability requires the reader to actually hold the misconception — Schroeder & Kucera (2022), moderator analysis — WR. This is the whole reason for the skip branch.

### Wrong model — where the error assigns the target another category's properties, say the category changes

- Misconceptions placing a concept in the wrong ontological category resist incremental correction — Chi (2005), *J Learning Sciences* 14(2) — MOD, influential. The finding is about the properties ascribed, not about what the thing is called.

### Start at the reader's question, not at the foundations

- Subject experts predict the formally simpler item is pedagogically easier, which inverts for novices; teachers' difficulty orderings inverted for algebra word problems vs. symbolic equations — Nathan & Petrosino (2003), *AERJ* 40(4); Nathan & Koedinger (2000) — MOD. Establishes that formal priority predicts learnability poorly; it does not establish a general entry level.

### Name the components and what each is for, then show how they interact

- Pre-training: d = 0.78, 10/10 — Mayer (2021) — WR within one research program.
- Advance organizers in Ausubel's sense: 135 studies, mean d ≈ 0.21, with meta-analytic estimates ranging 0.21–0.89 — Luiten, Ames & Ackerson (1980), *AERJ* 17(2), 211–218 — MOD. Supports preferring component naming; it does not establish that an abstract preamble is useless.

### Decompose where separable, stage the re-integration, skip it where the reader holds the pieces

- Isolated-elements effect: temporarily removing interaction helps and costs an explicit integration phase — Pollock, Chandler & Sweller (2002), *Learning & Instruction* 12 — WR.
- Segmenting reverses with expertise — Spanjers et al. (2011) — WR.

### Put the explanation at its referent

- Split attention / spatial contiguity: d = 0.82, 9/9 — Ayres & Sweller (2014); Mayer (2021) — WR.
- Signaling the structure explicitly: d = 0.69, 15/16 — Mayer (2021) — WR.

### Draw the picture when relations are what is being explained

- Multimedia effect d = 1.35, 13/13; coherence (exclude extraneous) d = 0.86, 18/19 — Mayer (2021, 2022) — WR within one research program. Supports relational pictures over decoration; the effect sizes do not establish diagrams as the strongest available intervention in general.

### Analogy — element-by-element mapping, stated breaking point, re-description in the target's terms

- Unhinted analogical transfer ~30% (unaided baseline ~10%); with an explicit hint, ~75–90% — Gick & Holyoak (1980, 1983), *Cognitive Psychology* — WR. This is what the mapping requirement buys.
- Retrieval runs on surface similarity while transfer runs on structure, so an unbounded analogy produces confident wrong inference — Gentner (1983), *Cognitive Science* 7(2); Holyoak & Koh (1987); Ross (1987) — WR.
- Leaving the analogy as the reader's durable representation produces reductive bias — Spiro, Feltovich, Coulson & Anderson (1989) — MOD.
- Comparing two analogs (schema induction) raised spontaneous transfer to ~52% — Gick & Holyoak (1983) — WR; established for analogs that both map, which is the stated condition.

### Two examples varying on surface, structure constant; trace one completely without a schema, contrast pair with one

- Worked-example effect for readers without a schema — Sweller & Cooper (1985); Renkl (2014) — WR.
- It reverses: problem-solving beats studied examples once a schema exists — Kalyuga, Chandler, Tuovinen & Sweller (2001), *JEP* — WR for the reversal; it does not establish the contrast pair specifically as the replacement.
- Novices generalize along surface features, which is what varying surface while holding structure counteracts — Ross (1987); Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981) — WR.

### Cut extraneous material, not mechanism

- Coherence (exclude extraneous) d = 0.86, 18/19 — Mayer (2021) — WR. It licenses cutting extraneous material, not compressing mechanism.
- People prefer simpler, fewer-cause explanations even against probability — Lombrozo (2007), *Cognitive Psychology* 55 — MOD. Reader preference is therefore not a guide to how much to cut.

### Name the terminal inference as the reader's to draw, and stop there

- Generation effect d ≈ 0.40; retrieval practice g ≈ 0.50 — Slamecka & Graf (1978); Bertsch et al. (2007); Rowland (2014); Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017) — WR.
- Self-explanation g = 0.55 (69 effects, 64 reports, n ≈ 5,917); scaffolded prompts beat open ones — Chi, Bassok, Lewis, Reimann & Glaser (1989), *Cognitive Science* 13; Bisra, Liu, Nesbit, Salimi & Winne (2018), *Educ Psych Rev* 30(3), 703–725; Berthold, Eysink & Renkl (2009) — WR.
- Several difficulties raise retention while lowering immediate performance and subjective fluency — Bjork & Bjork (2011); Bjork (1994) — WR as a family, DISP in parts (disfluent fonts failed replication). This means a reader's fluency judgment can diverge from their learning; it does not predict that a given explanation will feel worse.
- Explaining drives generalization and also overgeneralization from insufficient evidence — Williams & Lombrozo (2010), *Cognitive Science* — MOD. Pair a generative prompt with a counterexample where the reader could overreach.

### One turn, and it closes; no comprehension check, and assent is not evidence

- Confidence in explanatory understanding drops sharply once a step-by-step causal account is demanded — Rozenblit & Keil (2002), *Cognitive Science* 26(5), 521–562; Alter, Oppenheimer & Zemla (2010), *JPSP* — WR for the core effect, MOD for scope. The recorded boundary excludes facts, procedures and narratives. This grounds what assent is worth, not a check run here: verifying the reader's own account is `/grasp`'s contract.
- The illusion tracks the conflation of functional with mechanistic understanding — Rozenblit & Keil (2002) and later mechanistic-vs-functional prompt work — MOD.

### Deliberately not carried

- **Modality** (spoken narration alongside graphics, d = 1.00) — inapplicable to written explanation, and it vanishes for self-paced or high-prior-knowledge readers (Tabbers et al. 2004).
- **Redundancy** (avoid a second self-sufficient rendering) — d = 0.10, 8/12, the weakest of Mayer's set. Too weak to instruct on, and deliberate paraphrase for a reader without the schema stays defensible.
- Mayer's table is largely one research program's own tests; his 2021 individual-differences recasting makes prior knowledge a formal boundary condition on all of those principles.
