# Sources — scholarly orientation

For a question the scholarly record answers: what a study found, how a method performs, what a field holds.

- **Authoritative for a claim**: the paper itself, or its publisher or repository page. A survey or a citing paper is evidence of what the paper is said to show; open the primary page before resting a claim on it.
- **Which version**: a preprint, a conference version, and a journal version can differ. Cite the version whose text was opened, and carry the claim from that text.
- **Empirical effects**: where checkable, find whether the effect replicated and whether the paper was retracted or corrected. An effect that failed replication is reported as a caution, not as a law.
- **Absence and novelty** ("no study has…", "first to…"): such a claim reaches only as far as the searches made for it; carry what was searched beside the claim.
- **Details**: a volume, page range, date, or figure that no opened source gave is recalled, not cited; leave it open for a check.
