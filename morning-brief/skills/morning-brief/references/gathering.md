# Gathering

Read at steps 1 and 2 of the procedure. Establishing what is the case is `/inquire`'s; this file says what the brief needs established and what counts as a candidate.

## What the brief needs established

- **What the user is responsible for**, what that work depends on, and what their organization is pushing on now — enough to rank the user's projects. The ranking settles only after every connected source has been read: the busiest source is not the most important one.
- **What is still open to the user.** Each tool is asked for it directly wherever it can answer, with no date bound. Only where it cannot is the tool read back by time, past where the user was last keeping up. The previous brief supplies candidates and never bounds the read.
- **Updates**, sought by what the user's work depends on, not by where the user speaks.
- **The calendar**, to a fixed horizon: the next day, and on the week's first brief the rest of the week. Dated events in the user's own records count as occasions too.

No turn waits on an answer before the brief is saved. Of what `/inquire` hands back as the user's, only a decision someone is waiting on in a record is a to-do candidate, naming the decision and who is waiting. Any other question for the user, and an uncertainty about this run's own reading that the rules here do not settle, goes to the closing.

## Candidates

A candidate is anything that might be a to-do or an update:

- a to-do may come from something the user committed to, something asked of them, something an occasion requires of them, or an act a record names them for;
- an update is news, not the user's own act or message, that bears on their projects or their organization's priorities. An action that news implies for the user is a candidate of its own.

A candidate's source is the record where its current state lives and where the user would act on it, not the message that pointed there.

Every candidate is recorded with its source and ends with a verdict and its reason: to-do, push forward, open but not today, update, or drop. A verdict given before the checks is provisional. Every candidate whose verdict places it is shown, and a section of candidates holds no others.
