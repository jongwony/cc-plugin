# Visual page

Read at step 6 of the procedure. The page lowers the reader's load: a relation the brief's text states is shown as a picture, taken in at a glance instead of assembled from sentences.

## Figures

- A figure is drawn only for a relation among items that the text states and its layout does not already show: their order in time, who waits on whom, how measured quantities compare.
- Quantities the sources measured and the brief compares are drawn as lengths, each value beside its length and the scale stated. Quantities of one kind share one scale in one figure; another kind sits beside as a value or in a figure of its own. A derived quantity a judgment rests on appears beside its inputs.
- Every element of a figure comes from a verified item, points back to it, and keeps that item's standing — fact or estimate — shown by more than color. Before publishing, each element is checked against the item it points to.
- The page's text is the self-checked brief itself.

## Page

- The host's guidance for artifact pages and for data visualization is loaded and followed where the host offers it. Where it does not, a figure stays plain and legible, and nothing is told by color alone.
- A figure stays whole at any width: it re-lays out to fit, without scrolling inside itself or clipping a label.
- Text wraps where its language breaks: a language read in word units keeps each word whole.
- One page serves every run, and its address is kept in `<dir>/artifact-url`. The run replaces that page's content with this brief; a host step that requires reading the page first is met by reading it, not by merging. Where there is no address, or the page at it cannot be updated, the run creates a page and writes its address there.
- The page stays private; the run shares it with no one.
