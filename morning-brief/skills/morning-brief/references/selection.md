# Selection

Read at steps 0 and 3–5 of the procedure, and by the `morning-brief:refresh-brief` skill.

## Verified

A candidate is verified when this run has read its source record to its most recent activity.

- A fact is used as fact only from the record that owns it. Who a record names — asker, owner, counterpart — is such a fact. Who decides a schedule is such a fact, from the record that names that role; the schedule's owning record is their latest statement. Where none names one, the conflicting statements are shown with authors and times, and neither is written as fact.
- Any other carrier — a relay, a transcript, a summary, someone's mention — points there. What it carries keeps that attribution, with its time, wherever it is written, titles included.
- The owning record is reached through records. Where no route reaches it, a condition only a carrier speaks to holds at the carrier's standing: the item stands in its section, marked unverified at its owner. Unreached is for a condition no record speaks to.

## To-do

A to-do is listed only while all three hold at its source's current state:

1. **Open.** The action is not yet done, resolved or answered — by the user or by anyone, in this record or in an adjacent tool. Completion is the thing delivered, decided or handed over; an acknowledgement, a reaction or a promise is not. Where part is discharged, only the part still owed remains.
2. **The user's.** They were asked, or they committed, or a record names them for the act, which needs no ask. A record naming a group — more than one possible actor — or their authorship only infers it; a role, repository or channel the record shows only the user holds is the user. The user's own record of a commitment is a commitment. An occasion makes a to-do only where the record shows what the user owes to it; attending is not owing.
3. **Owed today.** A record shows another's work held on the act now — said by them, or set in that work's own state, queued behind it or marked blocked by it — or a loss that falls today. Instances, not its full extent: a deadline today, at the working day's end where it has no hour; a fresh ask to the user by name since their last act on the item; a date the user gave someone else, which is that person's deadline and stays owed each day until delivered or re-dated; a later step owed today that stalls on it. A date the user set only for themselves, once past, is a fact and not today's, and a gate nobody has reached yet is structural. A relative date resolves against its record's timestamp.

Every candidate that could be the user's open act gets an independent check, whatever its provisional verdict: a fresh-context subagent tests each condition both ways. Candidates that one record read settles share a check, and the steps of one chain are settled together. The checks run in parallel, and none is skipped to save time.

- **Hand-off**: the three conditions and the read-to-latest-activity criterion; who the user is; the current time; the candidate with its source, its history and any mark the user left on it; and the chain it belongs to.
- **Return**: for each condition, held, failed or unreached, with the link of the record read and that record's latest-activity time; and the action confirmed.
- A return counts only as far as it shows a record read to its latest activity.
- All three held: a to-do, listed as the action confirmed. A step owed today because a later one is stands only as far as that later one stands.
- Open and the user's, not owed today: push forward where what a later request to this assistant could produce would change what the user can do before the item's next dated point, or today where it has none; otherwise open, not today.
- Open or the user's failed: dropped, with its reason. An action dropped because the user's part is only inferred stays visible as an estimate on its news.
- A condition unreached: checked once more, then named in the closing with its provisional verdict, its source link and the missing condition.

## Shape

In this order:

- **Opening** — what the reader must know before choosing their first act, one line each, each in its own standing.
  - The to-dos already costing someone, then those whose cost begins before the day's next fixed occasion; a chain by its first open step; within a tier, the To-dos order.
  - A choice the day or a later fixed point forces on today's time, and an estimate or open question that would change the first act.
  - Where there is none, one line says so.
- **To-dos** — every to-do, as a checklist, in order of impact on what the user is responsible for. A chain's steps keep their order. Urgent wording raises nothing: what makes an item today's is given as the fact.
- **Push forward** — each framed as the next step toward what the record shows it serves.
- **Open, not today** — every open act that is the user's under condition 2 and not owed today. One line each, with the fact that keeps it out of the to-dos. Where its standing has gone stale — past due, nothing waiting — the decision about it is put as a question beside its line.
- **Updates** — news that is not the user's own act or message.
  - It is shown while it still changes the reader's day or has changed since it was last told: an event once; a state while it holds, restated in one line with since when.
  - A dated change says when it lands. One ahead that bears on the user's work is a state until it lands.
  - A measured quantity is news only when its change moves the item's standing against the line it is held to.
- **Today** — the day's occasions from the user's own calendar, in time order. A conflict among them is stated with the choice it forces.
- **Ahead** — the next day's shape; on the week's first brief, the rest of the week; and the occasions the user's open work leads to.
- **Closing** — what bounds this brief's coverage and what only the user can supply: the as-of time; how far back each read went, and what was covered of a source too large to read whole; the sources that failed, with what was tried; what ran without a dependency a step names; what no route could read or no check could reach; questions for the user; and what left the previous brief with no place here, or that none was found.

Today and Ahead carry no verdicts; another person's state appears only beside an item that depends on that person. Each item is placed once, with the project or area it belongs to; the opening and Ahead restate by title. An empty section is left out, except to-dos and today, which say that they are empty.

## Writing

- An item opens with its point in a short title: an update with what changed; a to-do with the next act given the current state, the person it concerns, and what it is due to where the record shows it. An item the reader can judge from that ends there. Where a record admits more than one way to discharge an obligation, such as delivering or re-dating, the to-do names the obligation and those ways.
- The body carries what changes the reader's next choice — the order of acts, the exception that would reverse it — beside the act it bears on, and ends where that choice stops changing. Values the brief compares are written compactly in the text, and a figure draws them. Reference figures and history stay behind the link.
- Wherever an item is restated, a figure included, it names the same act, the same owner of the remaining decision, and the same standing. A decision left to the user is not rewritten as an instruction, and a claim gains no certainty by being repeated.
- A list holds only parallel entries that answer one question, numbered where their order matters. A course of events the reader must follow stays a short paragraph that keeps cause with result.
- A thing is called by its name in `<language>`; an identifier sits beside the name where the reader needs it to act, and never replaces it, except for content its source restricts (`SKILL.md` §Boundaries).
- Each item ends with a link to its source record.

## Statement and estimate

The brief keeps what a source states apart from this run's own reading, in three standings: stated, proposed, estimate.

- A claim keeps the standing its source gives it.
- A reading of this run's own is written only where it changes today's act or says which of the user's work a piece of news touches. It is marked as an estimate in `<language>`, beside the observed facts and the source link it rests on. One held weakly says what would settle it.
- Facts or items placed together invite a relation. It keeps the standing the records give it, and the brief says so where no record states it; a record this run may read that could settle it is read first. Where the run can read for one of several competing claims and not the others, it says so beside them: what reach supplies is not support.
- An inferred owner, cause, duty, deadline or commitment is never written as fact, and never makes a to-do by itself.

## Continuity

The previous brief is the latest one saved in `<dir>`, read as of its latest refresh checkpoint where `morning-brief:refresh-brief` appended any. Its items are candidates again: nothing carries over without today's check.

- The saved file keeps the user's words verbatim and visibly apart from the run's own: every checkbox is written unticked, and a carried note is quoted and attributed beside its item.
- A tick or a note is the user's report. Where the record speaks, the record decides. A record is silent on an act only where the act would leave no trace in it: where the thing delivered would appear in a record read to its latest activity and does not, the record has spoken. Where it is silent, the report stands for the user's own part, and what it says of another person's act stays attributed to the user until that person's record shows it.
- Where an item's standing differs from the previous brief, the fact that moved it is stated where the item now stands, or in the closing where it has no place. A tick the record confirms needs neither.
- A to-do still open is listed as it stands now, with since when it has been open, not in the previous wording.
