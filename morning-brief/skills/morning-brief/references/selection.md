# Selection

Read at steps 0 and 3–5 of the procedure.

## Verified

A candidate is verified when this run has read its source record to its most recent activity. What announces an item — a notification, a preview, a list row — shows the ask, not its state.

- A fact is used as fact only from the record that owns it. Who a record names — asker, owner, counterpart — is such a fact. A schedule's owning record is the latest statement by whoever decides it.
- Any other carrier — a relay, a transcript, a summary, someone's mention — points there. What it carries keeps that attribution, with its time, wherever it is written, titles included.
- The owning record is reached through records, never by a command. Where no route reaches it, the latest carrier's statement is shown, marked unverified at its owner. Only a candidate with no carrier at all stays out of the sections.

## To-do

A to-do is listed only while all three hold at its source's current state:

1. **Open.** The action is not yet done, resolved or answered — by the user or by anyone, in this record or in an adjacent tool. Completion is the thing delivered, decided or handed over; an acknowledgement, a reaction or a promise is not. Where part is discharged, only the part still owed remains.
2. **The user's.** They were asked, or they committed, or a record names them for the act, which needs no ask. A record naming a group, or their authorship, only infers it. An occasion makes a to-do only where the record shows what the user owes to it; attending is not owing.
3. **Owed today.** The record shows someone bearing the cost of waiting now, or a loss that falls today. So: a deadline today; a person or agent who said they are waiting, or asked after it; a date the user gave someone else, which is that person's deadline and stays owed each day until delivered or re-dated; a later step owed today that stalls on it. A date the user set only for themselves, once past, is a fact and not today's. Nor is a step that is only structurally required, with no sign that anyone is stopped. A relative date resolves against its record's timestamp. An occasion already past leaves only what the user still owes after it.

Every candidate that could be the user's open act gets an independent check, whatever its provisional verdict: a fresh-context subagent tests each condition both ways. Candidates that one record read settles share a check, and the steps of one chain are settled together. The checks run in parallel, and none is skipped to save time.

- **Hand-off**: the three conditions and the read-to-latest-activity criterion; who the user is and their team; the current time; the candidate with its source, its history and any mark the user left on it; and the chain it belongs to.
- **Return**: for each condition, held, failed or unreached, with the link of the record read and that record's latest-activity time; and the action confirmed.
- A return counts only as far as it shows the source record read to its latest activity; the rest is unreached.
- All three held: a to-do, listed as the action confirmed. A step owed today because a later one is stands only as far as that later one stands.
- Open and the user's, not owed today: push forward where this assistant's output would change what the user can do before the item's next dated point; otherwise open, not today.
- Open or the user's failed: dropped, with its reason.
- A condition unreached: checked once more, then named in the closing with its provisional verdict, its source link and the missing condition.
- An action that surfaces any other way, another check included, is a new candidate with its own check.

## Shape

In this order:

- **Opening** — what the reader must know before choosing their first act, one line each: the to-dos whose cost is already being paid or begins before the day's first fixed occasion, those being paid first and a chain by its first open step; and any choice the day forces, estimate or open question that would change that choice, each in its own standing. Where there is none, one line says so.
- **To-dos** — every to-do, as a checklist, in order of impact on what the user is responsible for. A chain's steps keep their order. Urgent wording raises nothing, in a source or in the brief: what makes an item today's is given as the fact.
- **Push forward** — each framed as the next step toward what the record shows it serves, with who asked, why, and what has to happen.
- **Open, not today** — every open act that is the user's under condition 2 and not owed today, including work they started and left unfinished where the record shows it still matters. One line each, with the fact that keeps it out of the to-dos. Where its standing has gone stale — past due, nothing waiting — the decision about it is put as a question beside its line.
- **Updates** — news that is not the user's own act or message.
  - It is shown while it still changes the reader's day or has changed since it was last told: an event once; a state while it holds, restated in one line with since when.
  - A dated change says when it lands. One ahead that bears on the user's work is a state until it lands.
  - A measured quantity is news only when its change moves the item's standing against the line it is held to.
- **Today** — the day's occasions from the user's own calendar, in time order, with a note where this run has context that helps. A conflict among them is stated with the choice it forces.
- **Ahead** — the next day's shape; on the week's first brief, the rest of the week; and the occasions the user's open work leads to.
- **Closing** — what bounds this brief's coverage and what only the user can supply: the as-of time; how far back each read went, and what was covered of a source too large to read whole; the sources that failed, with what was tried; what ran without a dependency a step names; what no route could read or no check could reach; questions for the user; and what left the previous brief with no place here.

Today and Ahead carry no verdicts; another person's state appears only beside an item that depends on that person. Each item is placed once, with the project or area it belongs to; the opening and Ahead restate by title. An empty section is left out, except to-dos and today, which say that they are empty.

## Writing

- An item opens with its point in a short title: an update with what changed; a to-do with the next act given the current state, the person it concerns, and what it is due to where the record shows it. An item the reader can judge from that ends there, with its link.
- The body carries what changes the reader's next choice — the order of acts, the exception that would reverse it — beside the act it bears on, and ends where that choice stops changing. Values the brief compares are written compactly in the text, and a figure draws them. Reference figures and history stay behind the link.
- Wherever an item is restated — the opening, a title, another item, a figure — it names the same act, the same owner of the remaining decision, and the same standing. A decision left to the user is not rewritten as an instruction, and a claim gains no certainty by being repeated.
- A list holds only parallel entries that answer one question, numbered where their order matters. A course of events the reader must follow stays a short paragraph that keeps cause with result.
- A thing is called by its name in `<language>`; an identifier sits beside the name where the reader needs it to act, and never replaces it.
- Each item ends with a link to its source record.

## Statement and estimate

The brief keeps what a source states apart from this run's own reading, in three standings: stated, proposed, estimate.

- A claim keeps the standing its source gives it.
- A reading of this run's own is written only where it changes today's act or says which of the user's work a piece of news touches. It is marked as an estimate in `<language>`, beside the observed facts and the source link it rests on. One held weakly says what would settle it.
- An estimate names and marks the strongest relation its placement invites: the reader's inference, not only the one intended. Where a record this run may read could settle it, that record is read first.
- An inferred owner, cause, duty, deadline or commitment is never written as fact, and never makes a to-do by itself.

## Continuity

The previous brief is the latest one saved in `<dir>`. Its items are candidates again: nothing carries over without today's check.

- The saved file keeps the user's words verbatim and visibly apart from the run's own: every checkbox is written unticked, and a carried note is quoted and attributed beside its item.
- A tick or a note is the user's report. Where the record speaks, the record decides. Where it is silent, the report stands for the user's own part, and what it says of another person's act stays attributed to the user until that person's record shows it.
- Where an item's standing differs from the previous brief — gone, moved out of the to-dos, returned — the fact that moved it is stated where the item now stands, or in the closing where it has no place. A tick the record confirms needs neither.
- A to-do still open is listed as it stands now — what blocks it, the next act, and since when it has been open — not in the previous wording.
