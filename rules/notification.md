# Notification — what reaches choi's phone goes through aiward

- **A push exists to take load off choi's attention: it goes out where the work needs his attention and he would not otherwise turn to it when it matters.** Each push is judged on that need in the situation, so every push that arrives is worth reading.
- **A session sends through aiward, and aiward is what pushes choi.** `aiward-send` carries all three: a handoff as a `blocked` event, which aiward pushes as written; a check choi must make at a set minute as a request the aiward session schedules — both composed by the `ntfy-checkpoint` skill — and work that is done or stopped as a report, by the `aiward:report` skill. Where `aiward-send` is not on PATH the machine has no observer: the `ntfy-checkpoint` skill sends the handoff and the check with its own ntfy tools, and no report goes out.
- **A handoff to choi pushes at once, with the act ready and its surface open.** When the work stops on something only choi can do — a command to run (a `!` line), a permission grant, an act the classifier denied on every channel that reaches it, an approval it waits on, a credential or a consent on a page or in an app — the work is carried up to that act, and the surface it happens on is left open in the background for him to turn to. The push goes out with no delay in the turn that writes the handoff, naming the act, and the act's completion is read from that surface or the system it changes.
- **The reply that ends a session's work follows a report to the observer.** Before writing the reply that says the work it was given is done, or has stopped short of done on anything other than a handoff, the session reports itself through the `aiward:report` skill — work of any size, attended or not; a session given no work has no such reply and reports nothing. The observer reads the session's own record and decides whether to tell it to go on, push choi, or neither. A handoff goes out as its `blocked` event, with no report in its place.
- **A push that is superseded, moved or no longer needed is republished under its own sequence id, never cleared or deleted.** The `ntfy-checkpoint` skill carries each case.
- **A tap lands where that attention goes**: the page the act reads, and otherwise the owning session in the Claude app. Whenever a page takes the tap, the owning session stays one tap away as a `view` action. Through `aiward-send` the tap is the owning session, which holds the page's address.
