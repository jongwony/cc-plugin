# Visual page

Read at step 6 of the procedure. The page lowers the reader's load: a relation the brief's text states is shown as a picture, taken in at a glance instead of assembled from sentences.

## Figures

- A figure is drawn only for a relation among items that the text states and its layout does not already show: their order in time, who waits on whom, how measured quantities compare.
- Quantities the sources measured and the brief compares are drawn as lengths, each value beside its length and the scale stated. One kind shares one scale in one figure; another kind sits beside as a value or in its own figure. A derived quantity a judgment rests on appears beside its inputs.
- Each item on the page has an address. Every figure element comes from a verified item and points to that address.
- An element, and the relation a figure draws, keep the standing the text gives them, shown by more than color.
- Before publishing, each element is checked against its item, and where the host can show the page, it is looked at once.
- The page's text is the self-checked brief itself.

## Page

- The host's guidance for artifact pages and for data visualization is loaded and followed where offered. Otherwise a figure stays plain and legible, and nothing is told by color alone.
- A figure stays whole at any width: it re-lays out to fit, without scrolling inside itself or clipping a label.
- Text wraps where its language breaks: a language read in word units keeps each word whole.
- One page serves every run, and its address is kept in `<dir>/artifact-url`. The run replaces that page's content with this brief; a host step that requires reading the page first is met by reading, not merging. With no address, or a page that cannot be updated, it creates one and writes its address there.
- The page stays private; the run shares it with no one.
